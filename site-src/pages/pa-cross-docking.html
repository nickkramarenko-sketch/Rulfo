How cross-docking works in Harrisburg

Thirty doors on a 25,000 sq ft floor means inbound trailers and their outbound trucks can sit at the dock at the same time, with a short run across the floor from one to the other.

What arrives here is often port or long-haul freight bound for several Northeast and Mid-Atlantic receivers. If a pallet has shifted on the way, we fix it before it is reloaded instead of passing the problem to the next carrier.

Why Harrisburg for cross-docking

Middletown sits next to Harrisburg International Airport at the meeting point of four interstates: I-76 east to Philadelphia, I-83 south to Baltimore, I-81 north and south, and I-283 into Harrisburg. A trailer split here is about 2 to 3 hours from Philadelphia, Baltimore, Washington and New York.

Carriers use the dock to break a long-haul trailer into regional deliveries. Brokers book it when a load covers several receivers between New York and Washington. Importers bring freight in from the ports of Baltimore, Philadelphia and NY/NJ.

What freight can you handle?

Dry freight on pallets or off them: drums, crates, oversized or loose pieces and mixed loads. Our forklifts lift up to 4,500 lb, so call first about anything heavier, and about hazmat or other regulated freight.
